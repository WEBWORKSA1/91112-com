Heart Attack: Symptoms and First Aid

Recognise heart attack symptoms in men and women, call your emergency number, help the person rest, and prepare for CPR and an AED.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

A heart attack occurs when a coronary artery is blocked and heart muscle starts to die. It is different from cardiac arrest, but it can lead to one. Fast treatment saves heart muscle — “time is muscle”.

Common symptoms

  • Pressure, tightness, squeezing or pain in the centre of the chest lasting more than a few minutes, or that goes and comes back.
  • Pain spreading to one or both arms, the jaw, neck, back or stomach.
  • Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, light-headedness, unusual fatigue.

Women, older adults and people with diabetes more often have atypical symptoms — breathlessness, nausea, back or jaw pain, or extreme tiredness without classic chest pain. Take them seriously.

What to do

  1. Call your emergency number immediately. Don't wait to see if it passes.
  2. Help them rest in a comfortable position, often sitting on the floor with knees bent and back supported. Loosen tight clothing.
  3. Aspirin: dispatchers or local guidance may advise chewing an adult aspirin if the person is not allergic and has no reason to avoid it. Follow the dispatcher's instruction.
  4. If they have prescribed medication for angina (such as a nitroglycerin spray), help them take it as prescribed.
  5. Monitor breathing. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start CPR and send for an AED.

Do not let the person drive themselves to hospital. Paramedics can start treatment and transmit an ECG to the hospital en route.
