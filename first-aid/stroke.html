Stroke Signs: The FAST Test and What to Do

Recognise a stroke with the FAST / BE FAST test, note the time symptoms began, and call your emergency number immediately. Includes a symptom-onset timer.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

A stroke happens when blood supply to part of the brain is cut off by a clot or a bleed. Treatments that dissolve or remove clots work best in the first hours, so recognising a stroke quickly and calling for an ambulance is the single most important thing a bystander can do.

Use FAST (or BE FAST)

B — Balance

Sudden loss of balance, dizziness or trouble walking.

E — Eyes

Sudden blurred or lost vision in one or both eyes.

F — Face

Ask them to smile. Does one side droop?

A — Arms

Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?

S — Speech

Is speech slurred or strange? Can they repeat a simple phrase?

T — Time

Call your emergency number now and note the time symptoms started.

What to do

  1. Call your emergency number immediately — say “I think this is a stroke”. Do not drive them yourself unless told to; ambulances can alert the stroke team ahead.
  2. Note the time the symptoms began, or when the person was last seen well. This determines treatment options. Use our stroke onset timer.
  3. Keep them safe and comfortable. If conscious, sit or lie them with head and shoulders slightly raised. If unresponsive but breathing, place them in the recovery position.
  4. Give nothing by mouth — no food, drink or medication. Swallowing may be affected.
  5. Be ready to start CPR if they stop breathing normally.

Symptoms that disappear still count. A transient ischaemic attack (“mini-stroke”) can resolve within minutes but signals a high risk of a major stroke. Get urgent medical assessment the same day.
