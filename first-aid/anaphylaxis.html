Anaphylaxis: Severe Allergic Reaction First Aid

Recognise anaphylaxis, use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector, call your emergency number, position correctly and give a second dose if needed.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

Anaphylaxis is a rapid, severe allergic reaction that can close the airway or drop blood pressure. Common triggers are foods (peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish), insect stings, latex and medicines.

Signs

  • Swelling of the tongue, lips or throat; hoarse voice; difficulty swallowing.
  • Wheezing, persistent cough or difficulty breathing.
  • Dizziness, collapse, pale and floppy (in young children).
  • Often — but not always — hives, itching or flushing.

What to do

  1. Use the adrenaline auto-injector immediately into the outer thigh (through clothing is fine). Follow the device instructions.
  2. Call your emergency number and say “anaphylaxis”.
  3. Position: lie them flat with legs raised. If breathing is difficult, let them sit up. Do not let them stand or walk, even if they feel better.
  4. Give a second dose after 5 minutes if symptoms don't improve and a second injector is available.
  5. Start CPR if they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally.

Antihistamines do not treat anaphylaxis. Adrenaline is the first-line treatment — when in doubt, give it.
