Seizures: How to Help Someone Having a Seizure

Seizure first aid: stay calm, time the seizure, protect the head, never restrain or put anything in the mouth, and know when to call an ambulance.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

Most seizures stop on their own within a few minutes. Your job is to keep the person safe, time the seizure and know when it becomes an emergency.

During the seizure

  1. Stay calm and note the time it started.
  2. Protect them from injury. Move hard or sharp objects away. Cushion the head with something soft.
  3. Do not restrain their movements and never put anything in their mouth — people cannot swallow their tongue.
  4. Loosen anything tight around the neck and remove glasses.

After the jerking stops

  1. Roll them onto their side into the recovery position to keep the airway clear.
  2. Stay with them and speak reassuringly as they recover — confusion and tiredness are normal.
  3. Check for a medical ID bracelet or card, or a seizure action plan.

Call your emergency number if

  • It is their first seizure, or you don't know their history.
  • The seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, or seizures repeat without recovery between them.
  • They are injured, pregnant, diabetic, or the seizure happened in water.
  • Breathing is difficult or they don't regain consciousness.
