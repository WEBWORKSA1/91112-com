How to Build an Emergency Kit (72-Hour Checklist)

Build a 72-hour emergency kit for home, car and go-bag: water, food, first aid, light, power, documents and cash — with an interactive checklist.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

After a major disaster, help may take days to reach every household. Emergency agencies worldwide recommend that each household can be self-sufficient for at least 72 hours — many now advise up to a week or two of water and food at home.

The core kit

💧 Water

About 4 litres (1 gallon) per person per day, for drinking and basic hygiene. Store at least 3 days' worth; add purification tablets or a filter.

🥫 Food

Non-perishable, no-cook foods your household actually eats: canned goods, nut butter, crackers, dried fruit, ready meals. Pack a manual can opener.

🔦 Light & power

Flashlight or headlamp, extra batteries, power bank, crank or battery radio for official alerts.

🩹 First aid

A stocked first-aid kit, 7-day supply of prescription medication, spare glasses, and copies of prescriptions.

📄 Documents

Copies of IDs, insurance, medical records and contacts in a waterproof pouch plus an encrypted digital copy. Some cash in small notes.

🧰 Tools & hygiene

Whistle, multi-tool, duct tape, dust masks, wet wipes, rubbish bags, sanitary supplies, warm blanket or emergency foil blankets.

Personalise it

  • Babies: formula, bottles, nappies, wipes.
  • Older adults: extra medication, mobility aids, hearing-aid batteries, a list of medical conditions.
  • Pets: food, water, leash/carrier, vaccination records.
  • Climate: rain gear and warm layers, or sun protection and extra water for hot regions.

Where to keep kits

Keep a full kit at home, a smaller go-bag by the door for evacuation, a car kit (water, snacks, blanket, jumper cables, charger, first aid) and a mini kit at work. Check water and food expiry dates every six months — a good habit is to do it when clocks change or on a fixed date like New Year's Day.
