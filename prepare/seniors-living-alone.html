Safety for Older Adults Living Alone

Keep older relatives safe at home: prevent falls, choose a medical alert system with fall detection, organise medications and set up daily check-ins.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

Falls are a leading cause of injury for people over 65, and the outcome often depends on how long the person waits for help after a fall. A few changes cut the risk dramatically.

Fall-proof the home

  • Remove loose rugs and clutter; add grab bars in the bathroom and rails on both sides of stairs.
  • Install bright lighting and night-lights between bedroom and bathroom.
  • Review medications that cause dizziness with a doctor or pharmacist; get eyes checked yearly.

Medical alert systems

A personal emergency response system (PERS) — a pendant, wristband or smartwatch with a help button — connects the wearer to a monitoring centre or family. Compare: automatic fall detection, in-home vs. mobile (GPS) coverage, 24/7 monitored vs. unmonitored, battery life, water resistance, contract terms and total monthly cost.

Daily routines

  • Agree a daily check-in call or message.
  • Keep a list of medications, doctors and conditions on the fridge and in the wallet.
  • Make sure they can call the emergency number from their mobile while it's locked, and that a neighbour has a spare key.
