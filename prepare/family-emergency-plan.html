How to Make a Family Emergency Plan

Create a family emergency plan in 30 minutes: emergency contacts, meeting places, out-of-area contact, evacuation routes, roles for each person and practice drills.

Emergency? Call 112 now. Dispatchers will guide you through these steps on the phone.

In a crisis, phone networks overload, roads close and families are often apart — at work, school or travelling. A simple written plan prevents panic and lost time.

  1. Know your local numbers. Make sure everyone, including children, knows your country's emergency number and how to call it from a locked phone.
  2. Choose an out-of-area contact. Local lines may be jammed while long-distance calls and text messages still work. Everyone checks in with the same person.
  3. Pick two meeting places: one just outside your home (for fires) and one outside your neighbourhood (if you can't return home).
  4. Map evacuation routes from home, work and school, with at least two ways out.
  5. Assign roles: who grabs the go-bag, who collects children, who checks on an elderly neighbour, who is responsible for pets.
  6. Record medical needs and keep an ICE card in every wallet and backpack.
  7. Learn your school and workplace plans — where children will be taken and how you'll be notified.
  8. Practise twice a year: a fire drill and an evacuation drill. Time it.
